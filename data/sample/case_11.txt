A 55-year-old male with no prior cardiac history presents to the emergency department with severe chest pain rated 8 out of 10 on the pain scale.
The pain is crushing in nature, radiating to the left arm, and started approximately 90 minutes ago.
He also reports mild shortness of breath and mild diaphoresis.
He denies any nausea, vomiting, or loss of consciousness.
An ECG showed ST-segment elevation in leads II, III, and aVF.
Troponin levels are pending.
He was immediately given aspirin 325 mg, sublingual nitrate, and oxygen, and the cardiology team was activated.
